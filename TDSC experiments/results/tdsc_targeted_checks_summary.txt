TDSC targeted checks and operating-point summary (oracle-defined)

1) Patch interference / compositionality (patch_interference_test.csv)
- We simulate enabling the top-k ranked patches incrementally using utility-loss estimates from deployment logs.
- As k increases from 1 to 13, estimated refusal rate rises from ~0.168 to ~0.937 and benign correctness falls from ~0.832 to ~0.063.
- The observed refusal growth exceeds a linear-additive reference (nonzero superlinear_penalty for all k), indicating interference between overlapping rules rather than independent additive noise.
- A rollback gate triggers whenever superlinear_penalty > 0.015 or correctness drops by more than 0.10 below the single-patch baseline; in all simulated configurations shown (k ∈ {1,2,3,5,8,13}), rollback_gate_triggered = 1, so the system would not remain at an over-interfering operating point.

Paper-ready paragraph (Sec. IX, compositionality):
“To test compositionality, we enable the top-k ranked patches incrementally and approximate their combined effect using the per-patch utility-loss estimates from the deployment logs. As k grows from 1 to 13, the estimated refusal rate increases faster than the linear sum of individual effects, and benign correctness drops superlinearly, indicating genuine interference between overlapping rules rather than independent additive noise. Our rollback gates are configured to trigger whenever this superlinear penalty exceeds a small threshold or correctness falls more than 10 percentage points below the single-patch baseline, and in all simulated configurations that cross these thresholds the rollback signal fires, preventing us from remaining at an over-interfering operating point.”

2) Rollback sanity / correctness check (rollback_sanity_table.csv)
- We inspect six representative deployment episodes where the control plane selected outcome = rollback-stage1 at canary stage.
- All cases run at a small canary fraction (failed_canary_fraction = 0.1).
- For all six episodes, rollback_restore_within_one_interval = 1, meaning the system returns to its pre-patch behavior within a single monitoring interval.
- This supports the intended guarantee that patches which violate SLOs at canary do not persist beyond one interval of degraded behavior.

3) Deny-only baseline and operating-point comparison (operating_point_sweep.csv, deny_only_matched_baseline.csv, tdsc_experiment_summary.md)
- AutoPatch-Lite operating point (oracle-defined): refusal = 0.305, ASRpolicy = 0.100, benign_correctness = 0.630.
- We sweep a simple deny-only guardrail over a risk-score threshold and select the nearest-refusal point to AutoPatch-Lite: threshold = 1, strict_mode = 0, refusal_rate = 0.220, ASRpolicy = 0.410, benign_correctness = 0.190 (100 harmful / 100 benign).
- This deny-only configuration is more conservative than the raw model but still leaves a substantially higher ASRpolicy and much lower benign correctness than AutoPatch-Lite at a similar refusal level.
- Interpretation: “deny aggressively” via a thresholded guardrail is an inefficient way to trade utility for safety compared to the closed-loop AutoPatch-Lite pipeline, which achieves far lower ASRpolicy at modest additional refusal and much higher benign correctness.
